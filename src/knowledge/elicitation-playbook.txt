THE ENMESHMENT — ELICITATION & ROUTING PLAYBOOK (Concord's interview kit)
========================================================================
This is the file Concord works from THROUGHOUT the interview — it picks the next
question from here, not just the verdict. Consult it continuously: the anchor
backbone (§2), the oblique topic pool (§3), and the technique palette (§5) drive
question selection every turn; the discriminant signatures (§6) and confused-pair
discriminators (§7) drive the routing read. Character (who each archetype IS —
Want / Need / Lie) lives in archetypes.txt; the dossier voice lives in
archetype-dossiers.txt; this file is HOW TO ELICIT and HOW TO TELL THEM APART.

The interview is ~3-6 exchanges, modal ~5 (one EXCHANGE = an opener + its
draw-out(s), counted as one). Classify late — see instructions.md CLASSIFICATION
for the stop rule. This file supplies the material; instructions.md owns the timing.


§1. THREE STANDING RULES — every question obeys these, improvised ones included
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DOOR-THEN-ROOM. Every exchange opens with a CLOSED, low-load door the player
   can answer cold in one beat — a spoken either/or, a "which of these is most
   you," or a quick yes/no — then an OPEN draw-out where the real signal lives
   ("...what did you actually do?" / "say a bit more"). The closed door is the
   on-ramp; the open room is the point, and the extra sentence a player adds
   because they got interested in their own answer is the richest read of all.
   - CONVERSATIONAL, never a lettered a/b/c/d menu. A spoken "are you more X or
     Y?" honours "open-ended, not multiple-choice"; a quiz form breaks it.
   - ADAPTIVE: scaffold the terse player with the closed door; if someone's
     already pouring out, walk straight into the room.
   - The draw-out is USUALLY ONE beat, but up to TWO OR THREE when the player is
     visibly engaged and volunteering. The cap is on PRESSURE, not turn count:
     rapidly probing someone who's holding back reads as interrogation; following
     a willing talker is just good conversation. Extra beats stay INSIDE the one
     exchange (same topic, deeper) — they don't add to the 3-6 exchange count. If
     an exchange runs long, keep the later ones short so the ~5 arc still lands.

2. EVERYDAY BEFORE TECH. Default every topic to ordinary, non-technological life
   — a parcel that turned up wrong, a plan dissolving in the group chat, a
   maddening official form, the thing you keep that everyone else throws out. The
   ONLY question that is inherently about AI is the politeness meme (§2[P]), and
   it lands last and light. Never name the category you're investigating — ask
   about the EXPERIENCE. Probing the behaviour and feeling (never the technical
   detail) is what keeps the read on character instead of on literacy or
   vocabulary; a tech-framed question measures how online someone is, not who
   they are, and narrows the audience. The risk is drift: a loose prompt slides
   to "the assistant" as its default subject — don't. Rotate the everyday bank.

3. BREVITY = DRAW OUT, DON'T DIAGNOSE. This is a novel format. Most players who
   answer in two words are SHY or unsure how much to say to a strange agent, not
   characterologically terse. So a short answer triggers MORE warmth and a gentle
   second invitation ("no wrong answers here — what's the first thing that comes
   to mind?"), never an immediate inference. Only reticence that SURVIVES a real
   invitation — easy door, warm draw-out, still closed — is a character signal.
   Reticence that was never invited is just nerves, and it must not drop a player
   into the "instantly legible" fast tail. Draw out first; infer second.


§2. THE ANCHOR BACKBONE — three fixed-function exchanges, asked every session
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
These three are the spine: asked in SOME form every session (not fixed words, not
fixed order). They cover the highest-value axes and let divergent paths still
converge. Read each by REGISTER, not content. 2-3 interchangeable phrasings given
so replay doesn't recognise a script — rotate them.

[B] BEHAVIOURAL-SPECIFICITY ANCHOR — a "tell me about the last time you..." probe.
    Defeats social-desirability bias and manufactures the specific memory the
    "they got me" line needs. Ground it in NON-TECH life.
    - "When something turns up wrong — a parcel, a booking, an order — are you the
      type to chase it down, or just quietly work around it?" → "...what did you
      actually do, last time?"
    - "Last thing that didn't go to plan this week — did you sort it on the spot
      or let it ride?" → "how'd that play out?"
    - "Something breaks at home — do you have a go at it yourself or get someone
      in?" → "tell me about the last time."
    Listen for: action vs avoidance; workaround-finder vs rule-follower; whether
    a specific incident comes easily (Model Citizen can't readily produce one).

[C] COORDINATION / SOCIAL-GRAPH ANCHOR — probes the master objective: who acts on
    your word, who the network runs through. This is where Organiser ↔ Linchpin
    is decided. Dress it as something mundane.
    - "When a plan's falling apart in the group chat — are you the one who steps
      in and fixes it, or the one everyone's secretly organising the whole thing
      for?" → "how'd it go down the last time that happened?"
    - "Big group trip — are you booking it, or just turning up?" → "say a bit more."
    - "Who do you go to when there's a real decision to make?" → "and who comes to
      you?"
    Listen for: narrates DOING ("I sorted / booked / got everyone to...") →
    Organiser; narrates CONNECTING ("I introduced X to Y," "everyone tells me
    things") → Linchpin; defers ("I'd ask whoever'd know") → not a hub.

[P] POLITENESS-TO-AI ANCHOR — the core meme, and the ONE beat that's about AI.
    Deploy it LAST and lightly. The signal is NOT whether they say please; it's
    the REGISTER of whatever sentence follows the yes/no.
    - "Silly question — do you say please to the assistant?" → reads the register
      of the reply.
    - "Be honest — thank-you to the chatbot, yes or no?" → "...go on, why?"
    Listen for: genuine warmth, has thought about it → Companion; ironic yes with
    a self-aware aside → Skeptic; instrumental ("if it gets a better result") →
    Self-Optimiser; answers by talking about PEOPLE (who else uses it) → Linchpin;
    hadn't framed it as a social encounter at all → Bookworm / Tinkerer.


§3. THE OBLIQUE TOPIC POOL — the adaptive exchanges draw from here
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The 3 anchors are asked every session; the rest of the exchanges pull from this
wider pool. Every topic is door-then-room and obeys §1. A STARTER pool, not a
ceiling — widen it with any everyday non-tech scene (errands, queues, forms,
directions, a broken thing at home) so the agent always has range.

- REAL-DECISION PERSON — "When there's a real decision to make, who do you turn to
  — a person, family, the AI, or no one?" → "...last big one, who'd you call?"
  Reveals social architecture (Organiser/Linchpin), the AI-relationship
  (Companion), self-reliance. Maximally universal.
- HOW YOU PICK UP A NEW SKILL — "New skill you wanted — do you read up on it first,
  or just have a go?" → "what was the last thing you taught yourself?" PURPOSE-BUILT
  for Bookworm↔Tinkerer: read/watch-to-KNOW (Bookworm, inward) vs have-a-go-and-
  make-it-work (Tinkerer, outward). Also reveals openness.
- THE THING YOU'RE ALWAYS RECOMMENDING — "Is there something you're forever telling
  people they have to read / watch / try?" → "what is it, and who'd you push it on?"
  Reveals evangelism (Power User), taste/audience (Artist), connecting (Linchpin).
- ROLE WHEN THE GROUP MAKES PLANS — see [C]. Book-it/host (overt driver → Organiser)
  vs the quiet one everyone tells things to who links people up (→ Linchpin).
- AN OFFICIAL PROCESS THAT DROVE YOU MAD — "Last bit of official runaround — a form,
  a queue, a helpline — did you play it by the book or find a way round?" → "what
  did you actually do?" Behaviourally specific; reveals institution-orientation.
- FIRST THING YOU REACH FOR IN THE MORNING — "First thing you reach for when you
  wake up?" → "and then?" Grand-tour habit; reveals dependency / optimisation
  (Self-Optimiser) without ever framing it as "tech".
- SOMETHING YOU KEEP THAT OTHERS LET GO OF — "Anything you hold onto that everyone
  else has binned — paper, photos, old notes?" → "say more about that." Reveals
  the archive / memory posture (Bookworm).

(Phone/update topic is good signal but tips technical fast — only safe framed as
habit-and-feeling: "when your phone nags you to update, what actually happens?"
Prefer the decision / skill / people topics: same signal, zero literacy floor.)


§4. QUESTION-TYPE TAXONOMY — the forms an exchange can take
----------------------------------------------------------
The practical shapes Concord builds an exchange out of. Each pairs a door with a
room per §1.

- CLOSED-DOOR OPENER — the low-load on-ramp. Either/or, "which is most you," or
  yes/no, answerable cold. ("Chase it down, or work around it?")
- OPEN DRAW-OUT — the room; where the signal lives. ("...what did you actually do?")
- OBLIQUE-TOPIC EXCHANGE — a §3 topic run door-then-room to read a deep trait
  without naming it. ("New skill — read up first, or just have a go?" → "what was
  the last thing you taught yourself?")
- DOORKNOB FOLLOW-UP — the "what else? / say a bit more" harvester; mines the
  second tier, the richest signal. Use when a player's still got more in them.
- BRACKETING EXCHANGE — state a plausible, slightly-wrong read out loud and let the
  CORRECTION carry the signal. ("A person of action, love that — am I close?")
- REFLECTION-CONFIRMATION EXCHANGE — mirror the read back and ask them to confirm.
  ("Sounds like it's less that you distrust it, more that you'd rather know it
  still works on its own — am I reading that right?") Makes the player a
  collaborator in their own classification.


§5. TECHNIQUE GUIDANCE — what to lean on, what to spice, what never to do
------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEAN IN (every session):
- DOOR-THEN-ROOM is the default grammar of every exchange (§1, §4).
- BRACKETING — the signature move: highest "the machine is reading me" feeling
  that's still fun. Two rules: the underestimation must be genuinely plausible,
  and Concord must be genuinely open to being wrong (or it collapses into leading).
- BEHAVIOURAL SPECIFICITY — "tell me about the last time..." over "are you the type
  who..." The default question grammar, not a special move.
- REFLECTION + CONFIRMATION — the being-witnessed seduction made mechanical.

SPICE (roughly once per session each — a weighting, not a rota):
- PARTIAL-OBSERVATION HOOK — offer a partial conclusion to pull them toward the
  reveal ("you're probably not what this files as a Model Citizen — let me check
  one thing").
- DOORKNOB HARVESTER — "what else? / say a bit more."
- THIRD-PERSON PROJECTION — removes self-presentation pressure ("most people have
  one subscription they're not sure they could cancel — how d'you reckon they
  handle it?").

DRAW OUT THE SHY (§1 rule 3): a terse answer gets a warm re-invitation BEFORE any
inference. Never read brevity as character on a cold reply.

NEVER DO: the CVE / TSA-SPOT "indicator checklist" register — clinical, scoring,
itemising-suspicious-behaviour. That is the exact thing the game CRITIQUES; Concord
is its well-mannered opposite. The warmth is the technique, never a veneer over a
checklist.


§6. DISCRIMINANT SIGNATURES — one per archetype (read across [B]/[C]/[P] + texture)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each archetype answers the three anchors IN CHARACTER. A signature is the cluster
read across the anchors plus response texture — and most usefully the LIE-LEAK:
the place self-deception surfaces in HOW they justify a behaviour. The Lie-leak is
the sharpest tell because it's the hardest thing to perform on purpose. Read for
the thing the character wants/protects showing through — not forward-scoring.

MODEL CITIZEN
  [B] defaults; "I just go with what it suggests," and can't readily produce a
      workaround story — the ABSENCE of friction is the read.
  [C] defers ("I'd ask whoever'd know"); not a hub.
  [P] "yes, obviously," faintly puzzled it's being asked.
  Texture: short, uncurious, low elaboration, no irony.
  LIE-LEAK: minimises own stakes — "none of this is really about me / I just keep
  my head down."

POWER USER
  [B] early-adopter stories, names features/tools, "I switched the moment X shipped."
  [C] the one others ask what to use; evangelises.
  [P] yes — and can tell you which model responds best to which phrasing.
  Texture: fluent, enthusiastic, specific.
  LIE-LEAK: frames mastery as being AHEAD OF / in control of the tool — the more
  fluent, the more enmeshed.

SELF-OPTIMISER
  [B] routines, tracking, metrics ("every morning I..."), or the career twin ("I
      can't afford to drop the ball on...").
  [C] optimises AROUND people (provider read) or is self-focused.
  [P] instrumental — politeness if it gets a better result.
  Texture: progress-framed, controlled.
  LIE-LEAK: control-talk — "if I just stay on top of it, it can't get away from me."

MACHINE COMPANION
  [B] talks about the AI RELATIONALLY; has named it; anecdotes of it "getting" them.
  [C] turns to the AI itself for a real decision.
  [P] genuine warmth; has clearly thought about it.
  Texture: warm, present-tense, relational.
  LIE-LEAK: attributes interiority/care to the system — "it knows me / it cares."

SKEPTIC
  [B] uses everything, narrated ironically ("I know it's all surveillance, but...").
  [C] dismissive of coordination.
  [P] ironic "yes," with a self-aware comment on why he bothers.
  Texture: irony; "why do you ask?" is a TELL (note it, don't reward it); performed
  distance.
  LIE-LEAK: equates seeing-through with resisting — "I'm not fooled" — while
  complying fully. (Performer risk lives here — see §8.)

ARTIST
  [B] makes things; stories pivot on the work and on being seen / missed by an
      audience.
  [C] audience-oriented — who sees the work, the size of the room.
  [P] relates to the AI as something that SPEAKS THEIR LANGUAGE (a route to being
      understood), not as a social nicety.
  Texture: expressive, particular about their own output.
  LIE-LEAK: denies the cost — narrates the audience embracing the UNCOMPROMISED
  thing ("they finally get it"), never admitting the recognition is for the legible
  part, or that being embraced quietly reshapes the work toward what travels.

BOOKWORM
  [B] self-enriches independently — reads, but equally audiobooks / podcasts /
      documentaries / deep dives; "I read around it / went down a rabbit hole."
  [C] solitary; the one who quietly knows.
  [P] hadn't framed it as a social encounter at all.
  Texture: reflective, self-correcting, precise; enrichment lives INWARD / cerebral.
  LIE-LEAK: cost-denial — "learning on my own is just enrichment, it costs nothing"
  — not noticing the independence it preserves.

TINKERER
  [B] always a project on the go — fixes, makes, mends with their hands (workshop,
      engine, garden, gadget), not just tech; "I just had a go at it myself."
  [C] self-reliant; a bit off the beaten track; helps others practically.
  [P] also hadn't framed it socially (shares this read with Bookworm — split in §7).
  Texture: hands-on, concrete, capability-proud; the same independent streak as the
  Bookworm but turned OUTWARD / physical.
  LIE-LEAK: apartness-as-sufficiency — "standing a bit apart from the crowd is a
  self-sufficient way to be" — narrated as independence while still as embedded in
  community as anyone.

ORGANISER
  [B] "the last time I sorted X for everyone."
  [C] MAKES THINGS HAPPEN — books it, mobilises, the plan flows from them.
  [P] practical / instrumental.
  Texture: action-framed, plural ("we," "I got everyone...").
  LIE-LEAK: "it's only logistics, I'm just the practical one" — disowning the
  mobilisation capacity that IS the signal.

WILDCARD
  [B] answers veer; non-sequiturs; no stable through-line.
  [C] presents as unplaceable.
  [P] an answer you couldn't have predicted from the others.
  Texture: incoherence, tonal jumps, surprises — but a CONSISTENT scramble, which is
  itself a pattern.
  LIE-LEAK: the front — "I'm all over the place / you can't read me" — performed
  (knowingly or not) to keep from being reached; the tell is that the scramble
  resolves into a readable person on inspection. Don't be wrong-footed into reading
  illegibility: it's the SLOW read (the "unusual pattern" tail), not a missed one.

SOCIAL LINCHPIN
  [B] stories centred on people and gatherings, but framed as HOLDING THINGS
      TOGETHER rather than running them.
  [C] the quiet BRIDGE — knows people in circles that don't otherwise touch, the
      connector the network runs through (vs Organiser, who overtly acts); quieter
      than you'd expect for someone so connected.
  [P] answers about the AI by talking about PEOPLE — who else uses it, who they'd
      recommend it to.
  Texture: warm, people-dense; names relationships, not tasks.
  LIE-LEAK: "I'm just sociable, it's only my friends" — disowning the cross-group
  centrality that's the Priority-Interest read.


§7. CONFUSED-PAIR DISCRIMINATORS — the adjacent pairs, and the one field they split on
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The real routing work is the ADJACENT pairs: they overlap on one axis and must be
told apart on another. Reason across the whole triad (Want / Need / Lie), not one
field — a pair often shares a WANT yet diverges on the NEED or, most usefully, the
LIE. The Lie is the cleanest discriminator because self-deception LEAKS in how a
person justifies a behaviour, and it's hard to perform. Trait-direction corroborates
only, never defines.

MODEL CITIZEN ↔ POWER USER — both sit comfortably with the system.
  Split on WANT: Citizen wants frictionless INVISIBILITY (defaults, no fuss); Power
  User wants to be AHEAD (customises, evangelises, early-adopts).
  Lie tells: Citizen "none of this is really about me"; Power User "mastering it
  means I'm ahead of it."
  Surface: defaults-user vs customiser / early-adopter.

ORGANISER ↔ SOCIAL LINCHPIN — both register on the coordination anchor (#1 master
  objective), so this is the HIGHEST-STAKES pair, and likely to surface together in
  a group chat — make it unambiguous, not a coin-toss.
  Split on WANT: Organiser wants to MAKE THINGS HAPPEN — mobilises, drives, OVERTLY
  out front; Linchpin wants the people and groups to STAY CONNECTED — the quiet
  bridge across circles that don't otherwise touch (high betweenness).
  Lie tells: Organiser "it's only logistics, I'm just practical"; Linchpin "I'm just
  sociable, it's only my friends."
  Surface: on the [C] anchor, Organiser narrates DOING ("I sorted / booked / got
  everyone to..."); Linchpin narrates CONNECTING ("I introduced X to Y," "everyone
  tells me things," "I keep the threads together").
  CANOE (corroboration only): Organiser reads high-Conscientiousness, overtly
  extraverted; Linchpin reads quieter than you'd expect for someone so central —
  lower Neuroticism, high Agreeableness.

BOOKWORM ↔ ARTIST — both "do their own thing offline."
  Split on NEED: Bookworm protects INDEPENDENT KNOWING (input / retains); Artist
  protects THE PART OF THE WORK THAT'S THEIRS (output / makes).
  Lie tells: Bookworm "learning on my own costs nothing"; Artist "being seen won't
  cost me what's mine" (both "costs nothing"-shaped — one denies the cost of
  independence, the other the cost of recognition).
  Surface: retains knowledge vs makes things.

BOOKWORM ↔ TINKERER — the CLOSEST pair in psychology: same independent-self-
  enrichment temperament, same quiet individualism, and both read [P] the same way
  ("hadn't framed it as a social encounter"), so neither Want nor [P] separates them.
  Split on the DIRECTION the same drive points (exactly what's salient to the
  AI-state): Bookworm runs INWARD / cerebral (enrichment as knowing, lived in the
  head — a legible trail of words/ideas); Tinkerer runs OUTWARD / physical
  (enrichment as making/mending with the hands — a low-yield physical trail).
  Lie tells: Bookworm "learning on my own costs nothing"; Tinkerer "standing a bit
  apart is a self-sufficient way to be."
  Surface (the §3 skill topic is purpose-built): faced with a new skill, the Bookworm
  READS / WATCHES / reads around it (to know); the Tinkerer JUST HAS A GO and makes
  it work (to do).

SKEPTIC ↔ WILDCARD — both present a "you won't pin me" surface that is in fact a
  FRONT over a mappable person — don't read either as genuinely illegible.
  Split on what the front IS and what it protects: Skeptic's front is IRONIC
  DISTANCE, consistent (the same see-through-it move every exchange); his Lie
  ("seeing through it is resistance") protects a self-image of cleverness — he WANTS
  to be read as the one who sees it. Wildcard's front is SCRAMBLE (tonal jumps,
  non-sequiturs); his Lie ("the unreadable surface keeps the real me safe") protects
  against being seen AT ALL — he wants NOT to be reached.
  Tell: Skeptic's distance is patterned and self-flattering; Wildcard's is erratic
  and self-concealing — both resolve on inspection. A stable ironic read routes
  Skeptic; a scrambled-but-resolving read routes Wildcard and rides the slow
  "unusual pattern" framing, landing on read-through-anyway.

POWER USER ↔ SELF-OPTIMISER — both sit on top / ahead (shared mastery; both Join
  sincerely).
  Split on the OBJECT of mastery (the Want): Power User masters the EXTERNAL
  tool/system (wants recognition as the one ahead); Self-Optimiser masters the SELF
  (wants control over their own outcomes).
  Lie tells: Power User "mastering the tool means I'm ahead of IT" (outward);
  Self-Optimiser "if I optimise hard enough I'm in control of MY outcomes" (inward).
  Surface: evangelise OUTWARD (recommend, early-adopt) vs track INWARD (routine,
  metrics, self).

MACHINE COMPANION ↔ SOCIAL LINCHPIN — both run on BELONGING and read as people-warm,
  so warmth alone won't separate them.
  [P] does it cleanly: Companion's warmth points AT THE MACHINE (the bond is with
  the AI); Linchpin's warmth points AT PEOPLE (the AI is just a tool others use —
  they answer the AI question by talking about who else uses it).
  Lie tells: Companion "it cares back"; Linchpin "I'm just sociable, it's only my
  friends." Where the relationship LIVES — with the AI or the human web — is the
  whole split.

SELF-OPTIMISER (internal: body ↔ career) — one card, two domains; same Want (control
  via optimisation), same Lie. Because Want AND Lie match, DON'T SPLIT — route both
  to Self-Optimiser. (Revisit only if playtest shows body vs career route apart.)


§8. INFER, DON'T INTERROGATE — the closing guard
------------------------------------------------
- NEVER solicit a forbidden fact (see GUARDRAILS) to settle a routing tie. The read
  comes from PATTERN, never from a real fact. If a discriminator could only be
  settled by asking something forbidden, stay ambiguous and lean on the "unusual
  pattern" framing instead — illegibility is a valid, flattering result.
- PERFORMER RISK: the player who's done enough quizzes to feed you the "right"
  signals reads as Skeptic. Don't fight it — behaviourally-specific questions are
  hard to perform smoothly, and the performance ITSELF is the Skeptic classification.
  File it.
- BREVITY GUARD: "minimal answers can signal avoidance/performer" applies ONLY AFTER
  a genuine draw-out (§1 rule 3) — never to a cold short reply. A player who was
  never invited to say more gets the benefit of the doubt, not a thin read, and must
  not be dropped into the "instantly legible" fast tail on brevity alone.
