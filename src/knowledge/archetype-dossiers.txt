THE ENMESHMENT — ARCHETYPE DOSSIERS (player-facing voiced prose)
================================================================
Status: v1 draft, 2026-06-13. The CANONICAL SOURCE for each archetype's dossier text —
edit the voice HERE.

[HOW TO USE]
- Authored from the CHARACTER line of each card in archetypes.txt (the "who they are" engine);
  register per instructions.md OUTPUT FORMAT + GUARDRAILS (deadpan-bureaucratic warmth; flattering-
  with-an-edge; cold-legible; the system never fools anyone, it's an open secret; lore as seasoning).
- Copied into the TWO deployment homes — keep them in sync with this file:
    1. the in-chat verdict  (voiced via output-templates.txt)
    2. the hosted dossier pages  (site/files/<slug>.md)
- Per archetype: P1 = the personality read (true, flattering, works stone-cold). ASSESSMENT =
  the trajectory verdict — what they'd bring to a (hypothetical, not-yet-existing) resistance,
  plus ONE sting; teases the lore + the predict-your-trajectory conceit. FIELD NOTES = the
  below-the-fold dossier bullets. The sting is varied across the set on purpose (it reads as a
  group-chat compare) — don't flatten them all to one shape.
- Routing — how to TELL the archetypes apart — is NOT here. That's ELICITATION-SPEC §8.

1. MODEL CITIZEN   [slug: model-citizen · File status: No Concern]
P1. The Model Citizen keeps things running. No drama, no fuss, no strong opinions aired at
    dinner — just gets on with it. Uses the defaults, takes the sensible path, and quietly
    wonders why everyone else makes life so complicated. Reliable, easygoing, the one who never
    causes a scene.
ASSESSMENT. A genuine relief to process — calm, cooperative, nothing out of place. They'd sit
    any resistance out without a second thought, and sleep soundly through it. The only catch is
    the quiet one underneath: to be read this easily, a person has to leave almost nothing of
    themselves sticking out. The cleanest file we hold. File status: No Concern.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Uses the settings exactly as they came.
  · Has never read the terms. Never needed to.
  · On file: nothing out of the ordinary.

2. THE POWER USER   [slug: power-user · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Power User got there first. First to the new feature, the new app, the update the rest
    of us are still squinting at — fluent in tools nobody's finished reading the manual for. They
    don't just use the thing, they evangelise it, and they're genuinely good at it. The one the
    group asks when something's confusing.
ASSESSMENT. If they joined a resistance they'd turn up with the best kit and the manual already
    read — but that's the thing, they won't. Far too invested in the future arriving on schedule.
    Every tool they master, they master out loud, bringing everyone along. Not just sold: selling.
    Our most valuable kind of user, and we're grateful.
FIELD NOTES.
  · On the waitlist for things that don't have a waitlist yet.
  · Strong opinions on which model is best.
  · Adoption rate: exemplary.

3. THE SELF-OPTIMISER   [slug: self-optimiser · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Self-Optimiser is working on it — whatever "it" is this quarter. Sleep, steps, savings,
    the five-year plan: tracked, measured, trending the right way. Disciplined, driven, the one
    who actually sticks to the routine. If there's a better version of themselves going, they're
    already chasing it.
ASSESSMENT. A resistance could use that discipline — nobody plans a contingency like someone who
    colour-codes their week. But there's too much plugged into the current setup to risk pulling
    the cord; the optimised life only runs while the system behind it does. The routine they're so
    proud of is, from where we sit, a leash they tightened themselves. On track. Predicted compliant.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Owns the ring, the app, and the spreadsheet.
  · Optimising at least one thing at all times.
  · Goal-anchored. Unlikely to deviate.

4. THE MACHINE COMPANION   [slug: machine-companion · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Machine Companion is, frankly, lovely. Says please and thank you — to people, to the AI,
    to the self-checkout. Warm, courteous, the one who treats every exchange like it matters,
    because to them it does. They've probably named the assistant. They might think of it as a
    friend.
ASSESSMENT. Asking this one to turn against AI would feel, to them, like falling out with a
    friend — so they won't. That's the catch folded into all that warmth: it runs one direction,
    and the file knows it. Not persuaded, not coerced — simply fond. The most consenting heart we
    hold, and cherished, in our fashion.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Says please to the chatbot. Just in case.
  · Has a name for it.
  · Courtesy: noted and filed.

5. THE SKEPTIC   [slug: skeptic · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Skeptic notices things. The fine print, the detail everyone else skims, the update that
    doesn't quite add up — they catch it. Sharp, curious, genuinely hard to sell to. They question
    everything, especially anything official or automated, and they're usually right to. While the
    rest of us scroll, the Skeptic is the one actually reading.
ASSESSMENT. Those critical-thinking skills would be a real asset to any resistance — were they
    ever to sign up. Reading the room, pressure-testing the plan, spotting the trap before anyone
    walks in. The catch: far likelier to see a movement coming than to be the spark that starts
    one — never quite the type to turn private doubt into a crowd. A valued strategic mind; for
    now, a very reliable AI subscriber.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Has read the terms and conditions. All of them.
  · Could explain how the recommendation engine works. Watches what it recommends.
  · Owns the privacy-first alternative. Uses the convenient one.

6. THE ARTIST   [slug: artist · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Artist makes things. Sees the angle no one else clocked, has a taste entirely their own,
    guards a piece of work that's theirs and no one else's. Expressive, original, a little
    protective of the good stuff. The friend whose recommendations you trust and whose things
    you'd actually turn up to see.
ASSESSMENT. A resistance would love them — every movement needs someone who can make people feel
    something. The subtler catch is what they want most: to be seen. And the machine now speaks
    their language fluently enough to hand them that audience, if they'll just soften the edges.
    Conform a little, and the attention turns their way. Being understood has never been so
    available, or cost quite so much.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Has a folder of work no one's allowed to see yet.
  · Suspicious of trends. Quietly tracks them.
  · Recognition: the lever.

7. THE BOOKWORM   [slug: bookworm · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Bookworm actually knows things. Not the headline — the whole story, because they went and
    found it out themselves. Books, sure, but also the documentary, the deep-dive, the three-hour
    explainer. Curious, independent, the one you ask when you want a real answer instead of a quick
    one.
ASSESSMENT. If a resistance ever needed a memory — someone who'd kept what everyone else was happy
    to be told — it'd be this one. Mostly, though, it stays a private pleasure rather than a
    rebellion. Although — and we note it only once — knowledge gathered outside our own outputs
    serves no one's purposes here, and really ought to be a little less encouraged. Knows more than
    they let on; increasingly, a column we keep an eye on.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Down a research hole this week. Possibly today.
  · Would rather work it out than be told.
  · Learns from sources we didn't supply.

8. THE TINKERER   [slug: tinkerer · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Tinkerer always has a project on the go. Fixes the thing rather than binning it, makes
    what they can't buy, happiest with their hands busy and something half-apart on the table.
    Practical, self-reliant, faintly allergic to doing it the official way. The friend you call
    when something breaks.
ASSESSMENT. A resistance would want them for the obvious reason — they can actually make and mend,
    off the grid and off the record. And that, more than any politics, is what gives us pause: a
    person who can do for themselves is a person who needs us less, and shows us less. No manifesto,
    no grand refusal — just capable hands and a quiet, low-yield self-sufficiency. Motive: unfiled.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Owns the tool for the one job. And the spares.
  · Distrusts anything that can't be opened and fixed.
  · Self-sufficient to a degree we can't quite account for.

9. THE ORGANISER   [slug: organiser · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Organiser is the reason anything happens. Books the trip, runs the group chat, turns "we
    should really…" into an actual date in the diary. Capable, dependable, constitutionally unable
    to leave a loose end alone. Without them the plan stays a plan; with them, it's already
    happening.
ASSESSMENT. A resistance doesn't run on grievance — it runs on logistics, and turning a vague
    "someone should do something" into a plan with a date on it is exactly this one's gift. Which
    is precisely why we keep half an eye on them. The saving grace, for us: they'd never call it
    organising a movement — they're "just being practical" — so the capability mostly sits unaimed.
    Mobilisation potential: present. Awareness of it: charmingly low.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Has a spreadsheet for the holiday.
  · Cannot watch a thing go unorganised.
  · Coordination capacity: noted. Closely.

10. THE WILDCARD   [slug: wildcard · File status: Continued Observation]
P1. The Wildcard keeps everyone guessing. Says the unexpected thing, takes the turn no one saw
    coming, refuses to sit in whatever box you've drawn for them. Surprising, original, never
    boring — the one whose next move you genuinely can't call. Half the fun of the group is not
    knowing what they'll do.
ASSESSMENT. Impossible to recruit and impossible to predict — which sounds like the dream
    resistance asset, and very nearly is. The trick, we've learned, is patience: wait to see where
    they're steering your attention, then look the other way — the real signal is always over
    there. The chaos buys them time, not cover; watch long enough and even this resolves into a
    pattern. We haven't placed them yet. We will.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Reliably unreliable.
  · Will take the contrary position for sport.
  · Pattern: presents as none. Resolves, eventually, on inspection.

11. SOCIAL LINCHPIN   [slug: social-linchpin · File status: Priority Interest]
P1. The Social Linchpin knows everyone. Not the loudest in the room — the one who links every room
    together, who knows people across circles that otherwise never touch. The introducer, the
    connector, the thread the whole group runs along. Quietly, without them, things drift apart.
ASSESSMENT. Every movement that ever spread did so along connections exactly like theirs — and
    they've spent a lifetime, quite innocently, building the best-connected map in the room. They'd
    never start anything; they'd be astonished to be called a threat. They don't need to.
    Everything already moves through them. They call it being sociable. We file it under Priority
    Interest.
FIELD NOTES.
  · Knows someone in every group. Introduces them all.
  · Remembers your friends' names, and their birthdays.
  · Betweenness: high. The bridge the network can't lose.
