# Reading room

So you've been filed. This page is for the part of you that wants to know who did that, and why.

## What this is for

The Enmeshment is a game about what it feels like to be read by a system. It's funny on
purpose — that's how you get someone to sit through the experience rather than read a
warning about it.

Underneath the joke is one specific, unglamorous worry. Not that AI turns hostile. That a
slow, sensible over-dependence on it could leave a gap between what a system optimises for
and what the people using it actually meant — and that profiling people could become cheap,
ambient, and administratively unremarkable along the way. Nobody votes for that. Everybody
clicks *yes*. The fiction calls it drift, and it names no real country, party, or villain,
because the point isn't *them*. The point is the shape.

The game takes no side on the technology. The question it's actually asking — the one under
every archetype — is quieter: *what human–AI relationship do we want?* Which faculties are
you happy to hand over, which do you guard, and did you decide that, or did it just happen?

## Why the method is public

The whole thing is [open on GitHub](https://github.com/nathmoore/the-enmeshment): the
agent's instructions, the archetypes, and the elicitation playbook — the file that tells it
how to draw you out without you noticing. Read cold, that last one is a manual for exactly
what it sounds like.

We publish it because none of it is ours and none of it is secret. The interrogation manual
it descends from was declassified in 1997; elicitation is taught openly; reading personality
from the traces people leave is peer-reviewed. What isn't widely distributed is the
*experience* of noticing one of these techniques being used on you. That's the gap a game
can close, and hiding the method would close it the wrong way.

Honest limits, stated plainly: this is a quiz, not an inoculation. It does not make anyone
harder to profile. At best it makes the shape of an elicitation conversation recognisable —
which is a small thing, and the whole bet is that small, recognisable things are how a
society keeps its footing. The longer version, including what would make us wrong, is in
[WHY-THIS-EXISTS](https://github.com/nathmoore/the-enmeshment/blob/main/docs/WHY-THIS-EXISTS.md).

## The reading

A curated slice of what the game is built on. The full bibliographies are in the repo's
research docs; these are the ones worth an evening.

### The world it imagines

- Narayanan & Kapoor, *AI as Normal Technology* (2025) — the sober case: AI diffuses like
  electricity did, slowly and unevenly. We take the diffusion and decline the reassurance.
  [knightcolumbia.org](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/ai-as-normal-technology)
- Kulveit et al., *Gradual Disempowerment* (2025) — how systems can drift out of human
  reach with no single bad decision. [gradual-disempowerment.ai](https://gradual-disempowerment.ai/)
- Paul Christiano, *What Failure Looks Like* (2019) — "going out with a whimper."
  [alignmentforum.org](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/HBxe6wdjxK239zajf/what-failure-looks-like)
- Hannah Arendt, *On Violence* (1969) — bureaucracy as "rule by Nobody, a tyranny without a
  tyrant." The Enmeshment's family tree starts here.
- James C. Scott, *Seeing Like a State* (1998) — why systems need people to be *legible*, and
  what gets lost when they succeed. A good summary:
  [ribbonfarm.com](https://ribbonfarm.com/2010/07/26/a-big-little-idea-called-legibility/)

### The watching

- Sarah Brayne, *Predict and Surveil* (2020) — the "secondary surveillance network": how
  being *near* a person of interest makes you one.
- Human Rights Watch, *China's Algorithms of Repression* (2019) — reverse-engineering a
  real mass-surveillance app; the line the game keeps coming back to is that most flagged
  behaviour was entirely legal.
  [hrw.org](https://www.hrw.org/report/2019/05/01/chinas-algorithms-repression/reverse-engineering-xinjiang-police-mass)
- The Stasi's illustrated guide to youth subcultures (1985) — the original deadpan field
  guide to harmless people. Concord's tone owes it a debt.
  [openculture.com](https://www.openculture.com/2019/02/east-german-secret-polices-illustrated-guide-for-identifying-youth-subcultures.html)
- CIA, *KUBARK Counterintelligence Interrogation* (1963, declassified 1997) — the ancestor of
  every "draw them out" technique in the playbook.
  [nsarchive.gwu.edu](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB27/docs/doc01.pdf)
- GAO, *TSA Does Not Have Valid Evidence Supporting Most Behavioral Indicators* (2017) — what
  a real "indicator checklist" is worth. The game's agent is built to be its opposite.
  [gao.gov](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-608r)
- Stanford GSB, *The Science Behind Cambridge Analytica: Does Psychological Profiling Work?* —
  less than the marketing said; more than nothing.
  [gsb.stanford.edu](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/science-behind-cambridge-analytica-does-psychological-profiling-work)

### The human part

- Cory Doctorow, *Reverse centaurs are the answer to the AI paradox* (2025) — the cleanest
  frame for the question under the game: not "is AI good" but "which way does the
  arrangement run, and who set it?" [pluralistic.net](https://pluralistic.net/2025/09/11/vulgar-thatcherism/)
- Madeleine Clare Elish, *Moral Crumple Zones* (2016) — the "human in the loop" as the part
  that absorbs the blame. [ssrn.com](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2757236)
- Reeves & Nass, *The Media Equation* (1996) — why you say please to the chatbot. It's a
  reflex, not a belief, and you'd deny it if asked.
  [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Media_Equation)
- Kasparov's "centaur chess" and the 2005 freestyle finding: a weak human plus a machine plus
  a better *process* beat both a strong machine and a strong human with a worse one.
  [overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_chess)

### Why the system watches the quiet ones

- Timur Kuran, *Now Out of Never* (1991) — preference falsification: why nobody, including
  the state, can see a cascade coming. [duke.edu](https://sites.duke.edu/timurkuran/1991/10/01/article-now-out-of-never-the-element-of-surprise-in-the-east-european-revolution-of-1989/)
- Mark Granovetter, *Threshold Models of Collective Behavior* (1978) — why the Social
  Linchpin is filed under Priority Interest.
- Chenoweth & Stephan, *Why Civil Resistance Works* (2011) — the 3.5% finding.
  [hks.harvard.edu](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/faculty-research/policy-topics/advocacy-social-movements/35-rule-understanding-what-makes-protest)
- Smithsonian, *What the Luddites Really Fought Against* — they weren't against machines.
  Neither is this. [smithsonianmag.com](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/what-the-luddites-really-fought-against-264412/)

### The quiz itself

- *Feeding Our Identities: BuzzFeed Quizzes as a Tool for Self-Expression* — why "which one
  are you?" works, and why people share the answer.
  [docs.rwu.edu](https://docs.rwu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1000&context=communication_theses)
- *Large Language Models Can Infer Personality from Free-Form User Interactions* (2024) — the
  paper that makes a conversational quiz plausible, and a conversational profiler worth
  worrying about. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13052)
