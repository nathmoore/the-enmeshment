# Human Roles Research — What Stays Ours

> **Purpose:** Grounding for one specific theme the game wants to *prime* on level two:
> that living with AI (surveillance-state dynamics included) means deciding **which
> faculties we defend as distinctly human** — deep thinking, taste, judgment, decision,
> care, meaning-making — and reckoning with the **blurry "enmeshed" boundary** where
> human and machine cognition already blend. This doc is the research behind priming
> that theme early in Concord's interview and building oblique questions on
> boundary-behaviours. It complements — does not duplicate — [WORLD-RESEARCH.md](WORLD-RESEARCH.md)
> (the surveillance/control spine) and [GAME-DESIGN.md](GAME-DESIGN.md) (pillars, the set).

The game's title names the exact thing this doc is about. **Enmeshment** isn't only the
state merging with its AI (WORLD/CANON); on level two it's the quieter merge already
underway in each of us — the everyday blend of our own judgment, taste, and attention
with a machine's. The scenario externalises a private question the player is already
living: *where do I end and the tool begin, and which parts would I mind losing?* This
doc finds that question precisely enough to seed it obliquely, without ever asking about
technology directly.

---

## I. Thesis distilled

Navigating AI is, underneath the economics, a series of **boundary decisions**: which
human faculties we choose to keep exercising ourselves versus hand to a machine that can
often do them faster and cheaper. The literature converges on a shortlist of faculties
people intuitively want to defend — deep/independent thinking, taste, judgment, the act
of deciding, care, and meaning-making — but converges *even harder* on the fact that the
boundary is **blurry and moving**: most real tasks are already **centaur tasks**, done
half by a person and half by a system, and the interesting question is not "human or
machine?" but "**which way does the arrangement run** — is the machine serving the person's
judgment, or is the person serving the machine's throughput?" (Doctorow's centaur /
reverse-centaur). The game's job on level two is to make the player *notice their own
boundary* — where they've quietly ceded a faculty, where they still insist on doing it by
hand — without ever framing it as a tech-literacy quiz. That noticing is the level-two catch.

## II. The properly-human roles — the shortlist

A shortlist of faculties people reach for when asked "what should stay ours?" Each is a
candidate *foreground faculty* for an archetype and a candidate axis for an oblique
question. None is cleanly machine-proof — that's the point; the blur is §III.

1. **Deep / independent thinking.** The capacity to *know for yourself* — to reason
   something through rather than receive the served answer. Jack Clark's warning that "the
   goal of seeking advice is not merely to receive validation" points at the erosion here:
   an always-available, agreeable answer-machine quietly substitutes for the harder work of
   thinking. (Clark, *Import AI 431*; the game's **Bookworm** is this faculty personified.)

2. **Taste / discernment.** Knowing what's good — which of a thousand generated options is
   the *right* one, and why. Taste is the faculty that survives when generation becomes free:
   when anyone can produce, the scarce thing is choosing well. (General discourse; strongly
   implied by the centaur-chess finding that *process* — good human selection over machine
   options — beats raw machine strength. Kasparov.)

3. **Judgment.** Contextual, situated wisdom — the "it depends" a rule can't encode. Clark's
   manic-friend analogy is the sharpest statement: he doesn't *comply* with a friend's drastic
   proposal, he exercises judgment shaped by "conditioning and subtlety," and the AI's lack of
   that divergence "is illustrative of the problem." Judgment is what a moral-crumple-zone
   human is nominally there to supply — and structurally prevented from supplying (Elish, §III).

4. **Decision / the act of choosing.** Distinct from judgment: the willingness to *commit* and
   own the outcome — to be, in Amodei's critic's phrase, "the author of your own existence."
   The faculty most at risk from *ceremonial* human-in-the-loop, where a person signs off on a
   choice the system already made. (Critique of *Machines of Loving Grace*; Elish.)

5. **Care.** Attention paid to a particular person because they're *them* — the faculty most
   people flatly refuse to hand over (a child, a dying parent, a friend at 2am). Widely argued
   to be irreducibly human because it's constituted by the human relationship, not the output.
   (General "what should stay human" discourse; Amodei locates meaning itself here: "meaning
   comes mostly from human relationships and connection.")

6. **Meaning-making.** Authorship of one's own life and the significance we assign to effort,
   struggle, and accomplishment. Amodei argues meaning survives economic obsolescence because
   it never came from economic value ("people greatly enjoy activities that produce no economic
   value"); his critics counter that his frame renders "the experience of struggle, and the
   conditions that give rise to meaning, invisible." Both halves are useful to us: meaning is
   the deepest faculty *and* the one most easily optimised past without anyone noticing.

7. **Coordination / collective judgment** *(the social faculty).* Not an individual capacity —
   the crowd's. Surowiecki: a diverse, independent, decentralised, aggregated crowd out-judges
   any expert or machine — *but only while its four conditions hold*, and they fail exactly under
   the pressure an anticipatory state applies (independence collapses into groupthink; diversity
   is netted and priced). This is the bridge from the private faculties above to WORLD's
   coordination spine: the master human faculty the Enmeshment prices is people **thinking together
   independently**, which is also the faculty most fragile to being watched.

> **Design read.** These seven are the palette. An archetype foregrounds one (or the *refusal*
> of one). A question probes which one a player instinctively guards. None needs naming to the
> player — you elicit the faculty through a mundane scenario (§V), never the word.

## III. The blurry line — the "enmeshment" boundary (the useful section)

This is where question-seeds come from. Everyday tasks people already do *half with a machine*,
where the human/AI split is live, unstable, and revealing. Each is quiz-buildable because the
*same task* can be done as a centaur (you drive, the tool assists) or a reverse-centaur (the tool
drives, you rubber-stamp), and **which way a player runs it is a signal.**

- **Writing the message you could have written yourself.** A birthday note, a condolence, a
  tricky reply to a friend. Do you draft and let the tool polish, let the tool draft and adjust
  the tone, or refuse the tool for this one because it "wouldn't be from me"? The refusal-line is
  pure taste/care signal — and everyone has a private one.

- **Deciding via the recommendation.** What to watch, read, cook, where to eat. Do you take the
  first suggestion, treat the list as a menu you choose from, or actively fight the ranking to
  find the thing it *didn't* surface? (Taste vs. served-answer; judgment vs. default.)

- **Remembering vs. looking up.** Directions in a familiar city; a fact you half-know; a friend's
  number. Do you still hold it yourself, or has the holding migrated out? (The Bookworm's faculty
  — independent knowing — measured behaviourally, not asked.)

- **Navigating a place.** Follow the blue line turn-by-turn, or keep a map in your head and use the
  tool as backup? A near-universal, non-tech-coded proxy for "do I still do this faculty myself?"

- **Trusting a number you didn't compute.** A quoted price, a risk score, a route's ETA, a "people
  like you also…". Do you accept it, sanity-check it against your own sense, or override it when it
  feels wrong? (Judgment vs. deference — the moral-crumple-zone question in miniature.)

- **The good-enough draft you shipped anyway.** Sending the thing the tool produced without redoing
  it yourself — and where your personal "not for this" line sits. (Reverse-centaur self-report,
  framed as a confession about being busy, not about tech.)

- **Care you wouldn't delegate.** The task you'd never hand off no matter how good the tool got —
  choosing a gift for someone specific, sitting with someone, the hard conversation. The *refusal
  itself* is the richest signal, and it's flattering to give (everyone has a line they're proud of).

- **Correcting the machine that's confidently wrong.** Do you push back, shrug and route around, or
  assume it knows better than you? (Independence/judgment under an authoritative source — the exact
  faculty Surowiecki says a crowd needs and a bubble destroys.)

- **The thing you still do "the long way" on purpose.** Handwriting, mental arithmetic, reading the
  whole thing instead of the summary, cooking from scratch, fixing rather than replacing. The
  deliberately-retained faculty — often the one a person is quietly proud of (Tinkerer territory,
  §V).

> **Why these work as quiz material.** Each is (a) universal and concrete, no jargon (satisfies the
> §IV.D accessibility rule in RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS); (b) obliquely diagnostic — the player thinks
> they're chatting about their week, not being profiled on a faculty; and (c) *centaur-axis-aware* —
> the answer reveals not whether they use tools but **which way the arrangement runs for them**,
> which is the level-two theme itself.

## IV. The centaur / reverse-centaur axis

The single most useful framing this research offers, because it converts a fuzzy anxiety ("AI is
taking over") into a **clean, non-partisan, non-doom axis** the game can sit any behaviour on.

- **Centaur** (Kasparov's original, from post-Deep-Blue *Advanced Chess*, 1998): a human assisted
  by a machine — human head, tireless body. The human decides *when and how* to deploy the tool.
- **Reverse-centaur** (Doctorow): a machine assisted by a human — "a frail and vulnerable person
  being puppeteered by an uncaring, relentless machine." The human is a peripheral, there to absorb
  what the machine can't do (and the blame when it fails).
- **The load-bearing finding** (Kasparov, 2005 Freestyle): *"Weak human + machine + better process
  was superior to a strong computer alone and, more remarkably, superior to a strong human + machine
  + inferior process."* **The process — how the human and machine are arranged — matters more than
  the strength of either.** That's the whole game on level two: not "is AI good or bad" but "what's
  the arrangement, and who set it?"
- **The accountability trap** (Elish, *Moral Crumple Zones*): the "human in the loop" is often a
  reverse-centaur by design — kept nominally in control precisely so they can "bear the brunt of the
  moral and legal penalties when the overall system fails," protecting the *system's* integrity at
  the operator's expense. Judgment is asked for and structurally prevented. (Doctorow's gloss: "a
  neck in a noose is also a human in the loop.")

> **What the axis implies about good vs. bad arrangements — and why it's tone-safe.** Doctorow's own
> framing is the guardrail-friendly one: the point is not that AI is bad but that the *same tool* is
> liberating as a centaur and degrading as a reverse-centaur — *"Real choice distinguishes empowering
> tools from exploitative systems."* This maps cleanly onto the project's Luddite correction (CANON
> §4.3): the thing to resist is never the technology, it's the *arrangement* — being made to serve the
> machine. That keeps the level-two point squarely on "**what human-AI relationship do we want?**" and
> away from anti-tech doom. The Enmeshment, on this reading, is a society-scale reverse-centaur that
> nobody remembers agreeing to (ties to CANON's "convenience, never by coup").

## V. Design implications for the game

### V.1 How to PRIME the theme early (obliquely, no tech-literacy required)

The theme must be *in the water* of the interview, never announced. Concord is warm, brisk, reading
you cold (CANON §2). Three priming moves, all compatible with the elicitation playbook:

1. **Open on a faculty, not a tool.** Concord's early turns should touch a §III boundary-behaviour
   framed as ordinary life ("what's something you still do the long way, even though you don't have
   to?") — priming "which faculties are *mine*" before the player knows that's the axis. It reads as
   friendly small-talk; it's the theme's foundation stone.
2. **Never say "AI," "tool," or "technology" first.** Let the player introduce the machine if they
   will; profile the *faculty and the arrangement*, not the gadget. This satisfies the accessibility
   rule and keeps the boundary blurry-on-purpose — the player fills it in with their own life.
3. **Use the centaur axis as the invisible scorer.** Behind Concord, sort answers on *which way the
   arrangement runs* for this person (they-drive vs. it-drives), and on *which faculty they guard*.
   That's the level-two read; it never surfaces as a question.

### V.2 Candidate question-seeds (everyday-framed, never asking about tech)

Written to be dropped into Concord's improvisation. Each targets a faculty (§II) via a boundary-
behaviour (§III); none names technology.

- **"Is there something you still do the long way round, even when there's a faster option — and
  you'd defend it?"** → deep thinking / taste / the deliberately-retained faculty. *Strong opener:
  flattering, universal, self-authored, primes the whole theme.*
- **"When something recommends the 'best' option to you, do you tend to take it, treat it as a
  starting point, or go looking for what it missed?"** → taste vs. served-answer; judgment vs.
  default. *Cleanly centaur-axis-scoring.*
- **"What's a thing you'd never hand off to anyone — or anything — no matter how good they got at
  it?"** → care / the refusal-line. *Richest single seed; the refusal is the signal and it's
  flattering to give.*
- **"When you get a number you didn't work out yourself — a price, a time, a score — do you just go
  with it, or check it against your own gut?"** → judgment/deference; the moral-crumple-zone question
  in plain clothes.
- **"Something you used to keep in your own head — do you still, or has it moved out?"** →
  independent knowing; behavioural, not confessional.
- **"When you make something and it comes out just... fine, do you ship it, or is there a line where
  'fine' isn't yours enough?"** → taste/authorship; surfaces the reverse-centaur "good-enough"
  self-report as a point of pride, not shame.
- **"When you're sure something's got it wrong, are you the type to argue with it, or route around
  it?"** → independence under an authoritative source (the Surowiecki condition).

### V.3 Mapping the theme onto the 11 archetypes (which faculty each foregrounds)

Each archetype already foregrounds a human faculty from §II — this makes the level-two theme
*already load-bearing in the set*, not a bolt-on. (Faculty read below; character/routing unchanged —
see [archetypes.txt](../src/knowledge/archetypes.txt) / ELICITATION-SPEC §8.)

| Archetype | Foreground faculty (§II) | Centaur read |
|---|---|---|
| **Model Citizen** | *(cedes all of them)* | terminal reverse-centaur — optimised toward the default until little distinct is left to read |
| **Power User** | fluency/mastery | *believes* they're the centaur ("in control of it"); the sting is they've consented on everyone's behalf |
| **Self-Optimiser** | decision/control | control-through-optimisation; the routine is the leash — the tool drives, dressed as self-mastery |
| **Machine Companion** | care | care *pointed at the machine* — the one-directional relationship; the deepest, warmest reverse-centaur |
| **Skeptic** | judgment (ironised) | thinks they're the centaur ("I see through it"); irony substitutes for the actual judgment — reverse-centaur who feels like a centaur |
| **Artist** | taste / authorship | the centaur who fears the moment "being understood starts to feel like being reproduced" |
| **Bookworm** | deep / independent thinking | the §II.1 faculty personified — knows-for-themselves, resists the served answer; the clearest centaur |
| **Tinkerer** | the deliberately-retained faculty | §III's "does it the long way on purpose," in the physical world; centaur by disposition |
| **Organiser** | coordination (overt) | wields collective judgment as *action* — the Surowiecki crowd, mobilised |
| **Wildcard** | *(refuses legibility itself)* | resists being scored on any faculty; the sting is the scramble is itself a pattern |
| **Social Linchpin** | coordination (connective) | the crowd's *independence-and-diversity* made human — high betweenness; the master social faculty |

> **Load-bearing takeaway for authoring.** The set already spans the faculty palette. When writing a
> dossier's sting, the centaur read gives you a sharp, non-doom edge: name the faculty the player
> guards, then show — warmly — which way their arrangement actually runs. That *is* the level-two
> catch, delivered as flattery-with-a-wince.
