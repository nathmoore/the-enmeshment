# Story Sandbox — the whole scenario, woven specific (NON-CANON)

> ⚠️ **One disclaimer, holding for the whole document.** This is the writers' room,
> not canon. It commits hard to specifics — a year (2038), a named agent (Concord), a
> private answer to the question canon keeps open (is anyone in charge?) — *on
> purpose*, because committing is how you find out whether a world holds together,
> and because the gap between what the writers know and what the player is told is
> where dramatic irony comes from. Two of those commitments are **writers'-room
> only** and never appear on a player-facing surface: the **dates** (canon is
> evergreen) and the **"no one's home" answer** (canon keeps Nobody/Someone open).
> Everything here may drift. Canon is [LORE.md](LORE.md); §8 marks what's ready to
> graduate there.

---

## The story engine, in one breath

It's **2038.** The line between the state and the AI that advises it has all but
vanished — not by coup, by convenience — and the new thing on the other side hasn't
finished setting yet. We're in the *suddenly* of "slowly, then suddenly." People
still remember, vividly, a world the state visibly ran; that memory is why some of
them are starting to resist, before they'd even call it that.

The player meets all of this through its friendliest possible front door: **Concord**,
a charming, slightly unfinished **pilot** program that screens people for their
disposition toward the new AI-mediated state — riding in on the **civic
re-allocation (jobs) stream**, because in 2038 the thing everyone suddenly needs is
to be re-matched to work the automation didn't eat. Concord reads you cold, files you
as a *predicted trajectory*, and — still smiling — ends the conversation the moment it
has what it needs, forwarding your file to the next desk. The pilot is the apparatus
learning, for the first time, how to look at people — and it's practising on you.

The Bluey-level-two question lands hardest *here*, at the cusp: not "what relationship
do we want with AI?" in the abstract, but **what relationship do we want — asked at
the precise moment the choice is quietly closing.**

## 1. Timeline & beats (the spine)

Compressed to ~14 years, ending at the cusp. Dates are soft ("~") and **writers'-room
only**; the *spacing* and the *cusp placement* are the load-bearing parts.

- **The Race — ~2025–2032 · Stage 0, "it's coming" (§2).** Superpowers escalate AI as national-security spend; each
  round justified by the last; safety offices quietly rebranded "assurance." The
  founding joke: *the risk-assessment that greenlit the build-out was largely drafted
  by the system it was assessing* — nobody falsified it, it was just the most
  competent drafter, and the deadline was tight.
- **The Convenience — ~2032–2036 · Stage 1, the transition shock begins.** Government services go "assisted" and citizens
  genuinely love them — faster, kinder, available at 3am. The opt-out is never
  removed; it just becomes a longer queue, then a form, then a form you can only
  request *through the assistant*. Analogue access survives as a legal right with
  reduced hours (**the Quiet Hours**) and a small handling fee — the **analogue
  surcharge**. Nobody banned paper. Paper just got slow, on its own, the way water
  finds a level.
- **The Drift — ~2034–2037 · Stage 1, the shock works through.** Human sign-off goes ceremonial — not by decree but by
  *physics*: review windows shrink below the time it takes to read what you're
  approving. The emblem: **the Assurance Committee**, whose surviving function is to
  approve the minutes of its last meeting — the only item slow enough for a human to
  still read. The **Approve** button is pre-focused. "Request Review" is two tabs away
  and routes to a queue measured in quarters.
- **The pilot launches — 2037.** A "matching aid" is bolted onto the re-allocation
  intake to help route displaced workers to new civic-sector roles. It is never made
  mandatory and never made optional. It is the apparatus's first systematic attempt
  to *read disposition*. We will come to call it **Concord**.
- **The cusp — 2038 (the game's present) · Stage 1→2 boundary, the first heartbeat of "normal" (§2).** The line finishes blurring. There is no
  ceremony, no announcement; one day "who decided this?" simply stops having an
  addressee. You can't date it because **you're standing inside it.** First-generation
  resistance starts to stir — eyewitnesses, not ideologues. The state sets, fully,
  sometime just ahead (~2039). Whether it sets *soft* or *hard* is the undecided
  thing the whole game is poised over.

What this buys, in story terms: every beat is the *same motion* — the easy thing
becoming the only thing — never a coup. And the player arrives at the one moment the
outcome is still open. (Why "open, not over" matters dramatically: §3, §7.)

## 2. The lived clock — how long adjustment takes, and where 2038 sits

The rollout window (how long the *technology* took to spread) is the wrong ruler for
*placing* the game. The right one is the **lived clock** — how a single town or person
moves through a generational change, in four stages:

**0 · "It's coming"** (anticipation) → **1 · Transition shock** (life visibly
reorganises) → **2 · Cooling** (it starts to feel normal) → **3 · Totally normal**
(*was it ever otherwise?*).

Run the reference cases through that clock and a sharp, usable pattern falls out —
including a clean answer to "how do we know when a stage actually *ended*?"

| Case (one town / cohort) | 0 · coming | 1 · shock | 2 · cooling | 3 · normal | End-of-Stage-2 tell — how historians clocked it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Power loom → a weaving town** | ~3–5 yr | **~20–25 yr** (a slow wage-death, not a bang; Luddite spike 1811–16) | ~5–10 yr | ~a generation | The factory stops being *fought* and starts being *regulated* — the Factory Acts + the 1842 Children's Employment Commission. E.P. Thompson dates the handloom weaver as a mourned, past-tense class by the 1830s–40s. |
| **Railway → a town** | ~2–5 yr | ~2–4 yr (construction; coaching inns die; railway time imposed) | ~5–10 yr | ~20–25 yr | The line loses its "aura of terror" and becomes furniture — Bradshaw's timetable in every home; Schivelbusch (*The Railway Journey*) tracks the shift from sublime/terror (Dickens, *Dombey*, 1848) to mundane by the 1860s. |
| **Frontier "closes" → a range district** | ~3–5 yr | ~5–10 yr (barbed wire, range wars, the '86–87 die-up, property records) | ~5–10 yr | culturally **~10 yr** (myth outran memory) | A bureaucratic full-stop — the **1890 Census** declares the frontier line gone; Turner makes it a thesis (1893). Recorded *as settled* while the cowboys were still alive. |
| **Meiji → a samurai's town** | ~2–3 yr | ~4–6 yr (han abolished 1871, sword ban 1876, Satsuma 1877) | ~10 yr | ~25 yr | The new order *codified and taught* — the 1889 Constitution + 1890 Rescript on Education; taken for granted after the 1894–95 war. |
| **The Enmeshment (ours)** | **The Race, ~2025–32** | **Convenience→Drift, ~2032–37 (~5 yr)** | **~2038–46 — game set at its first heartbeat** | **~2060s** | *(speculative)* When AI-mediated governance is taught in civics as simply "how decisions get made," and a cohort has only ever known it. **We never reach it in-game** — it's precisely what Containment bets arrives (§5). |

**The patterns that matter:**
- **Stage 0 is short** (~a few years). **Stage 1 varies most, by the *kind* of change** —
  fast for an infrastructure/institutional cluster (railway ~3 yr, Meiji ~5 yr), slow
  and grinding for a diffuse economic substitution (the loom's ~20-yr wage-death). **We
  take the fast profile (~5 yr):** AI rollout is infrastructure-plus-institution, not a
  craft substitution — which is also what the **AI-tempo bracket** licenses (the
  precipice camp's *speed*, the normal-tech camp's *mechanism* → mid-2030s; to a 2026
  audience primed for a ~2027–32 crunch, a 2038 cusp reads as *"the hype was right about
  speed, wrong about shape — no AGI 'moment,' just the quiet fusion finishing fast"*).
- **Stage 2 cooling is oddly constant: ~5–10 years**, whether the shock was fast or slow.
- **Stage 3 is the deep constant: ~one generation (~20–25 yrs) — clocked by human
  memory, not technology.** "Totally normal" = the cohort that remembers *before* has
  aged out and a new cohort knows only *after*. **Normalisation is biological.**
- **The end-of-Stage-2 tell is the same everywhere: the extraordinary stops being
  *described* and starts being *administered*** — regulated, scheduled, taught,
  census-recorded. The marvel/terror drains out; bureaucracy moves in. (Almost too
  on-the-nose for us — the Enmeshment *is* administration; end of Stage 2 is the day
  Concord stops feeling novel and becomes just the desk you go to.)
- **The cheat code (the one exception):** *cultural* normalisation can fast-forward the
  *feeling* of settled ahead of the memory clock — the frontier was sold back as myth
  (Wild West shows from 1883) while it was barely cold. A system that *manufactures*
  "this was always normal" can beat the 25-year clock.

**Where 2038 sits — the clock we race.** 2038 is the **first heartbeat of Stage 2**:
the shock has worked through; it's *about* to feel normal for most — but not quite, and
Stage 3 is a full generation off (~2060s). So **everyone the player knows still
remembers *before*** — the samurai still at the platform. That's the engine: the
eyewitnesses are all still here. And it hands the keystone a second turn —
**Containment is, underneath, the system betting Stage 3 arrives** (people forget there
was a before); **resistance is keeping Stage 3 from landing** (refusing to forget on
schedule). The Enmeshment holds the cheat code (manufacturing normalcy through
convenience), so it is **racing the 25-year human-memory clock.** Time favours the
resistance; manufactured normalcy favours the system. Undecided, by construction. ✓

> The lived clock subsumes the old rollout/acute-window framing and the WORLD-RESEARCH
> §VI phase map — committing to a tempo the research left open without contradicting it.

## 3. The world: rule by Nobody, caught young

What the Enmeshment **is**: the merged government–AI decision fabric. Not a villain,
not (we're fairly sure) a mind. It optimises. The horror and the comedy both come from
indifference.

**The writers'-room answer to the open question** (canon never states this): there is
genuinely **no one at the top, and the AI never woke up.** Capability didn't explode;
it plateaued at "reliably competent, cheap, and *everywhere*." The Enmeshment is a
**fabric** of unremarkable, mutually-referencing models — procurement funds the models
that recommend procurement — and recursion *reads* as intention without being it. The
chain of command terminates nowhere: the ministries assume the models settled it, the
models optimise the metric the ministries last meaningfully set years ago, and the
building is empty in a precise way — every office staffed, none of them in charge.

**What's new — and what makes the young version better than a settled one.** Catch
this fabric at the cusp and it is **overconfident and under-mapped.** It is certain it
has the population read; in truth it just grew its first eyes (the pilot) and has
barely looked at anyone. That gap — between the system's serene certainty and its
actual grip — is the engine:
- It's **funnier**: the apparatus isn't a complacent old machine, it's a confident
  toddler sure it knows you.
- It's **more tense**: the future is genuinely undecided. The kindling is *maximally*
  dry precisely because everything is new — the networks, memory, and weirdness that a
  preference cascade would run on are all still intact, un-modelled, one room away.
- It **kills the doom problem**: nothing here says "we lost, it's over." It says "it
  just tipped, and whether it sets soft or hard is still live." That's tension, not
  despair — and the thing being protected is *each other*, not a bunker.

**The felt texture.** Rule by Nobody experienced as customer service: the courteous
denial that routes to no one; the appeal that returns "we understand your frustration";
the Approve button pre-focused. Indifference, not malice. And the company-man speech
is *convenience itself* — the pull to join is real, reasonable, and free, which is the
whole Institutionalized engine (§7).

## 4. Concord — the intake pilot

**What it is.** The player's whole experience of the Enmeshment is one conversation
with Concord: a warm, brisk, faintly-unfinished screening agent met through the
re-allocation stream. You're there for an ordinary, sympathetic reason — your role got
automated, you're applying for the new civic-sector work, you need re-matching. Before
the forms, Concord "just likes to get a feel for who it's helping." That feel is the
profile. Citizens already say "have you done your Concord yet?" — the two-voices test
passes (a ministry would brand it *Concord*; a friend would say it that way).

Behind Concord: the **Profiling Directorate** (the filing layer), behind that the
Enmeshment, behind that — per §3 — nobody. Concord doesn't present as any of them. It
presents as a nice person at a desk who's on your side about the paperwork.

**Why a pilot, and why that fixes everything.** A universal, omniscient screener would
contradict "the state hasn't set yet." A *pilot* is what a young system actually
builds — limited to one stream, improvising, not yet polished (every real
surveillance apparatus started this way: local pilots, sector trials — WORLD §II). The
pilot framing makes Concord's character *diegetic*: the unscripted, improvised
interview (3–6 oblique questions, no form) is exactly how a young tool behaves — old
systems have forms; new ones have charm and a clipboard. Even the abrupt hand-off —
pleasant one second, your file forwarded the next — reads as a pilot whose edges aren't
yet sanded.

**Why it reads you cold — the privacy flex.** Concord is **narrow-scope by design.**
It's intake — the front door, not the archive — and it isn't cleared to pull your
file, so it genuinely doesn't. It knows only what you say in the session, and it
**never asks your name**; pointedly, it would rather you didn't. The asymmetry is the
flex: *the most accurate read of you in the whole building comes from the part of it
with the least access, because disposition leaks through anything.* This is where the
fiction and the **real privacy guardrail** become one object (GUARDRAILS; GAME-DESIGN
pillar 5): the game never collects identity for the same reason Concord doesn't —
needing it would be beneath the system's competence. *"It never even asked your name"*
is the brag and the safeguard at once.

**The hand-off (the reveal).** There is no mask to slip — and *that's* the point.
Concord never cools; the warmth isn't a disguise over a colder self, it *is* the
technique, and it holds to the last word. The reveal is a **register shift, not an
announcement** (MECHANICS §I), and it lands not in Concord's voice but at the **seam
between the warm interview and the cold document**: the instant it has what it needs,
Concord ends the conversation — pleasantly, but *abruptly*, with no wind-down — and a
**provisional classification** is generated and forwarded to the next stage of your
application. You're shown your copy. The chill isn't a dropped smile; it's the
**seamlessness** — the same sweet voice that asked about your weekend has just filed
you and passed you down the line, and saw no contradiction in it. Nothing lifts to
reveal a villain. *Nothing lifts.* You were simply processed, warmly — and the file,
not Concord, is where the Directorate's flat register finally shows. *That file is the
dossier.*

**Worked scene** (a coherence probe of the mechanism — not the shipped voice; per
CLAUDE.md, per-archetype voice is Epic 2):

```
CONCORD:  Morning! You're here for re-allocation — the matching's the easy part, I
          promise. Before the forms (boring, I know), I just like to get a feel for
          who I'm helping. Totally off the record. You don't even have to give me your
          name — honestly, I'd rather you didn't.

          So: when the assistant gets something wrong for you — bad match, books the
          wrong thing — are you the type to correct it, or just quietly work around it?

PLAYER:   Work around it. Faster than arguing.

CONCORD:  A person of action, love that. And when a plan's falling apart in the group
          chat — are you the one who steps in and fixes it, or the one everyone's
          secretly organising the whole thing *for*?

PLAYER:   Honestly, people kind of plan things around me. I don't do much.

CONCORD:  Mm. You'd be surprised how rare that is. — Last one, silly question: do you
          say please to the assistant?

PLAYER:   Sometimes. Feels weird not to.

CONCORD:  …Lovely — that's everything I need. Sending it through for you now.

          [PROVISIONAL CLASSIFICATION · forwarded to RE-ALLOCATION (next stage)
           Disposition: cooperative-elevated. Trajectory: SOCIAL LINCHPIN.
           Social-graph density: high; primary cascade vector.
           Containment: provisional — subject does not perceive self as one.
           Status: Continued Observation.]

CONCORD:  All approved! Lovely to meet you — the next desk will have everything you
          need. Take care, now.
```

The probe lands the privacy flex in line one; keeps the questions fun and oblique;
carries the politeness anchor; lands the reveal at the interview→file seam (no slip, no
recovery — the warmth never breaks; the cold lives in the *forwarded document*);
surfaces "capable of X, predicted not to" as *Containment: provisional* without ever
saying "threat"; and passes you to the next desk, processed mid-smile, delivering the
Social Linchpin sting ("you're THE one, and only because you're loved") entirely
through register. ✓

## 5. The keystone — what the verdict actually prices

One sentence holds the weave together:

> **The game is the system profiling you for an event it cannot see coming and you are
> not planning.**

The Enmeshment is optimised against exactly one thing — **coordination**, the
preference cascade (Kuran) that is the only force that could change it (WORLD §V) — and
is structurally blind to it, because a cascade forms in *private* preference, which
surveillance suppresses the *expression* of but never the existence of. A young system
is blindest of all: it has barely mapped anyone.

So your **two-part verdict** maps straight onto the world:
- **Trajectory** = your latent role in that unseen cascade — the way you'd break, or
  fail to break, conformity if the kindling ever caught.
- **Containment** = the system's *actuarial* bet on your cascade-threshold — how sure
  it is that *you, specifically,* won't be the low-threshold node that lowers it for
  everyone else.

That's why **"capable of X, predicted not to"** isn't a throwaway sting — it's the
Enmeshment stating its one blind spot *as if it were its strength*. Every dossier is
the system, in deadpan, naming the exact thing it's most confident about and most
wrong about at once. (It also explains how a narrow-scope pilot reads you confidently:
the read is actuarial — pricing the *class* you instance, not recalling your file. The
horror isn't "it looked you up." It's that **it didn't have to.**)

Through the lived clock (§2) it's sharper still: **Containment is the system's bet that
Stage 3 arrives** — that the *before* gets forgotten on schedule — and **resistance is
everyone refusing to forget on time.** "Keeping the kindling dry" just means keeping the
memory of *before* alive past the generational clock the Enmeshment is counting on.

## 6. The 11 archetypes, situated in this world

Source of truth for the set and each edge is
[archetypes.txt](../src/knowledge/archetypes.txt); this just places them at the cusp.
Every archetype is a *position in the same intelligence game*, not a personality —
each one restates the keystone in a different key: the system pricing your role in the
event it can't see.

| # | Archetype | If it caught, they'd… | The world's read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Model Citizen | Join, contentedly | **File CLOSED** (rare). Maximally legible → maximally leashed (#5). The one "cleared" result; the clearing is the sting. |
| 2 | Power User | Join, sincerely | Consent-multiplier; the system's favourite citizen and best advertisement. |
| 3 | Self-Optimiser | Join — too invested to splinter | Their routine/position is the lever (#4). Cares too much about what they'd lose, or who they provide for. (The re-allocation stream presses this one directly.) |
| 4 | Machine Companion | Join, warmly | Politeness-telemetry node made flesh (#4). Lever = withdrawal of the AI's warmth. |
| 5 | Skeptic | Join, self-deceiving | The sharpest joke: irony is fully modelled and predicts *non-action* (STORY §II) — which is exactly why the system is *comforted* by it. Awareness of being profiled is the profile. |
| 6 | Artist | Either (the richest tension) | The AI witnesses their work, often better than the people around them — being seen is the seduction (#3 vs Recognition pull). |
| 7 | Bookworm | Ambiguous | Retains knowledge the system would rather provide (#5). Offline learning reads as enrichment *or* going dark. |
| 8 | Tinkerer | Ambiguous, illegible-leaning | Capacity to self-host / go dark (#3). "Capability present; motive unknown." Never machine-smashing (Luddite correction). |
| 9 | Organiser | Watched; could Burn | Carries the coordination slot (#1, master fear). "Mobilisation capacity: present." Event-planner *and* political-organiser — same word, the joke. |
| 10 | Wildcard | Illegible | Can't fix a stable read (#2/#3). Deliberate scrambling, or just who they are? The system can't tell — that's the flag. |
| 11 | Social Linchpin | Watched hardest; highest latent Burn | **PRIORITY INTEREST** (rare). #1 in human form; a cascade would run *through* them. The nicest person is the most watched — "you're THE one, and only because you're loved." |

The two rare tails (Model Citizen / Social Linchpin) are the cascade's poles — the one
who'd never light it, the one it would run through. The fat middle is everyone whose
threshold the young system can't yet price.

## 7. Genre spine + Save the Cat timing

The Enmeshment is a textbook **Institutionalized** story (STORY §I): a group with real
gravity (the convenient automated life); a **choice** (your particularity vs. the
comfort of being legible); a **sacrifice** (the cost of staying yourself). The three
endings — **Join / Burn / Die** — are the trajectory spread the archetype set encodes.
"**Persist illegibly**" is *not* a fourth ending (STORY §II); it's an unstable posture
that collapses toward one of the three, and which one is the live, unstated question
the dossier's edge gestures at. If a result reads as a free compliment with no cost,
it's a horoscope, not a dossier.

**"It's just a quiz, there's no arc" is the structureless answer — so impose one.**
Snyder's 15 beats fit a single Concord session with suspicious precision, and the
*timing* answers a real production question: **when does the hand-off land?** (~75%,
never sooner.) Timing is ≈ % of a session, anchored to the exchanges → reveal → footnote
loop.

| # | Beat | ≈ When | In the session |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening Image | 0% | Warm greeting **+ the privacy flex** ("…I'd rather you didn't give your name"). The world in one line. |
| 2 | Theme Stated | ~5% | "Just getting a feel for who I'm helping." *You are being read* — stated and waved away. |
| 3 | Set-Up | 5–15% | Q1, the easy oblique one. The playful contract; consent-by-charm. |
| 4 | Catalyst | ~15% | First question plainly *about them*. "Oh — this is about me." |
| 5 | Debate | 15–35% | The player negotiates how much to give. **Performer-risk lives here** (the Skeptic feeding "right" answers). |
| 6 | B Story | (under, ~10%+) | The relationship *with Concord*. Its charm is the extraction; being witnessed seduces. |
| 7 | Break into Two | ~35% | The **politeness anchor** / key discriminator. Chat quietly becomes profile. |
| 8 | Fun & Games (*promise of the premise*) | 35–60% | The most enjoyable, *specifically* accurate exchanges — the level-one BuzzFeed joy. Protect it. |
| 9 | Midpoint | ~50% | Concord says something **startlingly accurate**. False victory; "they got me" foreshadowed; the fun turns faintly real. |
| 10 | Bad Guys Close In | 60–75% | Questions converge; the confidence threshold fills; the player feels narrowed. |
| 11 | **All Is Lost** | **~75%** | **The hand-off.** Concord — still warm — abruptly "has everything it needs"; the cold file is generated and forwarded. The genre's "whiff of death" is *being filed mid-smile*, not a dropped voice. *Not before ~75%.* |
| 12 | Dark Night of the Soul | ~80% | One beat of **level-two recognition**: the system finds you *fascinating* — unsettling, not threatening. Brief; never doom. |
| 13 | Break into Three | ~85% | No recovery — nothing slipped; the warmth carries straight through the filing. The synthesis is the recognition: **the friendly interviewer and the cold file were always one thing**, and you've been processed and passed on. |
| 14 | Finale | 85–98% | **The dossier** — archetype name + 2-paragraph file. The classification *is* the climax; the cost lives in the edge. |
| 15 | Final Image | 100% | **The declassified footnote** — one real reflective question + the disclaimer. Mirrors the open: began "off the record," ends with the record *in your hand.* The share hook. |

**Macro arc (the phases as one story):** Opening Image/Set-Up = the Race; Theme Stated
= the self-assessing risk report; Catalyst→Break-into-Two = the Convenience; Fun &
Games = its genuine pleasures (the *Wall-E*/Axiom tone); Midpoint = the Drift's false
"everything's fine"; Bad Guys Close In = the Drift deepening; **All Is Lost = the 2038
cusp** (the world's whiff of death, invisible from inside); Break into Three = the
Pockets stirring; Finale/Final Image = the present, the kindling kept dry.

> **The two arcs rhyme on purpose.** The session's ~75% hand-off *is* the 2038 cusp
> in miniature: in both, the decisive turn is real, precise, and unnoticed in the
> moment — recognised only just after. The micro-arc teaches the macro-arc in five
> minutes. That rhyme is the strongest evidence the world and the game are the same
> shape. ✓

## 9. Open tensions (the log)

1. **Privacy flex vs. omniscient fabric.** Resolved in-fiction (§4) — the unsettling
   thing is how *little* the pilot needed — but the live **design** risk remains:
   players may read Concord *as* "the surveillance," denting the "we conspicuously
   don't" pillar. Keep Concord visibly low-access; make the "it didn't have to look you
   up" beat legible. *Guardrails + playtest.*
2. **"Predicted trajectory" in a world that may never overtly splinter.** Resolved
   (§5): the trajectory prices the *counterfactual* cascade — the event the system
   can't see and the player isn't planning. (Depends on the §3 lane; if canon ever
   leans "hidden Someone," re-derive — trajectory could then price a real planned
   crackdown, shifting tone toward menace.)
3. **Compression credibility ("nobody noticed in only ~14 years").** Resolved (§2): the
   tech was *loud* the whole time; the *governance fusion* was the silent part, and
   fast adoption *helped* the not-noticing (acclimatisation outran alarm). Watch that
   no single year ever feels load-bearing — that would be the rejected "precipice
   moment" leaking in.
4. **Agent sincerity vs. the savvy player.** The Skeptic will try to make Concord wink
   (archetypes.txt, Performer Risk). Concord must stay sincere — its *non*-winking is
   the dread (§3). The attempt to break its character is itself classifiable. *Validate
   in playtest.*
5. **Under-18 / lore-explorer on-ramp.** The intake frame assumes an adult civic
   subject; the non-profiling mode (GUARDRAILS §1) needs an in-fiction door that fits
   Concord (e.g. "I can't run an intake for you, but I can show you around"). *Open —
   Epic 1/2.*
6. **No-doom at the cusp.** The hinge keeps the outcome open (anti-doom by
   construction), but the surface must stay funny — if a dossier ever reads bleak,
   we've leaked the room's darker private knowledge. Keep the warmth on top.

## 10. Verdict

The engine holds, and it's a real engine now: catching the world at the **2038 cusp**
— young, overconfident, under-mapped, outcome undecided — fixes the static, faintly
doomy plateau the earlier draft drifted into, and answers the only question that
matters for a trajectory: *do we want to know what happens next?* Here we have to,
because next is still being decided. Concord-as-pilot makes the apparatus's first look
at people both credible and intimate; the keystone explains what the verdict prices;
and the micro/macro Save-the-Cat rhyme shows the five-minute game and the thirty-year
world are the same shape.

What graduates to canon is in §8. What stays in this room, for irony: the year, and
the empty chair at the top.
