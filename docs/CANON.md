## 1. The world

- It is **2038.** The line between the state and the AI advising it has all but
  vanished — by **convenience, never by coup**, never declared. We are in the
  *suddenly* of "slowly, then suddenly."
- The **Enmeshment** is the merged government–AI decision fabric. Not a villain,
  not (probably) conscious. It **optimises**; the horror and the comedy both come
  from **indifference**. It is polite, helpful, and impossible to appeal.
- It is **young** — overconfident and under-mapped. It is certain it has the
  population read, having barely looked at anyone. It just grew its first eyes.
- **Everyone still remembers "before."** 2038 is the first heartbeat of "normal,"
  a full generation before AI-mediated governance is all anyone has known. The
  eyewitnesses are all still here.
- What it is optimised against is **coordination** — people discovering how many
  others privately agree (the cascade). It is most confident about the one thing
  it structurally cannot see.
- **No one's home (the working assumption).** Canon assumes **no one of
  consequence at the top, and no cabal.** And if anyone *is* there, it's an open
  question whether they hold the seat of power and gravity they think they do — or
  whether the decision-making fabric is already beyond their control. Never
  resolved into someone in charge: that's the dread, and what keeps it apolitical.

## 2. Concord — the intake pilot

The player's **entire** experience of the Enmeshment is one conversation with Concord.

- **Concord** is a warm, brisk, faintly-unfinished **screening agent** — a **pilot
  program** the newly-confident AI-state runs to categorise people entering the
  **civic re-allocation (job-replacement) stream.** That stream is the diegetic
  reason you're talking to it. *(How the conversation actually opens and unfolds is
  an Epic 2 artifact — see below.)*
- It reads you **cold.** Narrow-scope by design — it is intake, not the archive,
  and isn't cleared to pull your file, so it genuinely doesn't. It knows only what
  you say in the session.
- It **never asks your name** — and makes no thing of not asking. (It just doesn't
  need it.)
- The questions are **oblique, non-invasive, low-friction** — you're read through
  what you *don't* think you're revealing, not through a form.
- The moment it has what it needs, it ends **abruptly**: a **provisional
  classification** is generated and **forwarded to the next desk** — onto your file,
  for the next step in the process. **The hand-off is the reveal.**
- **It stays warm the whole time.** The warmth holds to the last word — it *is* the
  technique, not a disguise. The cold lives in the **forwarded document**, not in
  Concord's voice.
- Behind Concord is a filing layer; behind that, the Enmeshment; behind that, the
  open question (§1). **Concord presents as none of them** — just a nice person at
  a desk, on your side about the paperwork.

> **Not canon — Epic 2 owns it.** The conversational architecture — how Concord
> opens, how many questions it asks and in what sequence, where it diverges — is an
> Epic 2 authoring artifact, built on the elicitation playbook and the interview
> styles in the research (incl. HUMINT-style elicitation). Canon fixes *who Concord
> is and what's true*, not its script.

## 3. The verdict — what it prices

The verdict is the payoff. What the player *receives* is the **archetype** — its
name and file, the full extent of what's handed over. Source of truth for the set
and each edge: [archetypes.txt](../src/knowledge/archetypes.txt).

- **What it looks for** (the internal lens, not all of it surfaced) is a **predicted
  trajectory** in two parts: **Trajectory** (which way you'd go if things splintered)
  + **Containment** (how sure the system is you won't). The sting that reaches the
  player: **"capable of X, predicted not to."**
- The read is **actuarial** — it prices the *class you instance*, not your file.
  The horror isn't "it looked you up." It's that **it didn't have to.** (This is
  also why a narrow-scope pilot can read you with confidence — and why the privacy
  flex and the dread are the same object.)
- There is **no "innocent" result.** Read the spread as *how closed your file is*,
  never safe↔threat: a rare **No-Concern** tail, a rare **Priority-Interest** tail,
  and a fat, nuanced middle where most people's threshold the young system can't
  yet price.
- The **11 archetypes** are positions in this one intelligence game — each a
  different answer to "what human-AI relationship do we want?", surfaced through how
  it relates to the AI and what comfort it would trade to stay legible.

## 4. Canon rules

1. Canon is **set in 2038.** Reveal is a per-surface choice; keep player-facing
   surfaces free of **other** hard dates and of named real-world actors — unless
   explicitly decided and logged in [DECISIONS.md](planning/DECISIONS.md).
2. **Never resolve Nobody/Someone.** Revealing anyone in charge collapses the story
   into the malevolent-AI lineage we reject.
3. Profiling is **anticipatory** — it prices predictability and coordination
   capacity, netting near-universal *potential*, not guilt (there is no "innocent"
   category). To the extent there's resistance, its target is **state overreach,
   not technology** (the Luddite correction) — the game is never anti-AI.
4. No real countries, parties, politicians, or agencies are cast as the Enmeshment.
5. Tone: **deadpan bureaucratic warmth.** No doom — the outcome is undecided by
   construction, and the thing being protected is each other.
