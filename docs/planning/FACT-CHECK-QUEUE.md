## Copy-paste validation prompt (for a fresh conversation)

```
You are fact-checking a research brief for a fiction project. I will give you a list
of specific factual claims. For EACH claim:

1. Find the most authoritative PRIMARY or near-primary source you can (peer-reviewed
   work, official report, the originating document, or the named researcher's own
   publication — not blog re-summaries).
2. Rate it: CONFIRMED / OVERSTATED / WRONG / UNVERIFIABLE.
3. If OVERSTATED or WRONG, give the corrected, defensible version in one sentence.
4. Note source quality (primary / reputable secondary / advocacy / marketing) and any
   important scope caveat (e.g. "true of one city pilot, not nationally").
5. Be willing to say "I can't verify this" rather than guessing. Do not invent sources
   or citations; if you can't find one, say so.

Keep each answer to ~3–5 lines. Use web search. Group by my numbering.

CLAIMS:
1. China's "social credit system" is NOT a single unified national citizen score — it's
   a patchwork of local pilots plus national blacklists; the widely-quoted "389 rules,
   AAA–D tiers" scheme is the Rongcheng city pilot specifically.
2. China's internal-security ("stability maintenance"/weiwen) budget exceeded its
   external-military budget in several years around 2011–2014.
3. "Mass incidents" in China rose from ~8,700 (1993) to ~180,000 (2010); the 180,000
   figure originates as a Sun Liping (Tsinghua) estimate, not an official statistic.
4. The Stasi employed ~90,000 staff and ~189,000 registered informants (IMs) in a
   country of ~16 million (≈1 in 63 as a snapshot); cumulative estimates over the
   regime's life are far higher (claims of ~1 in 6–7 total collaborators).
5. China's Xinjiang IJOP system flagged people via ~36 behaviour types (plus ~50 apps),
   the majority of flagged behaviours were legal, and most flags were for relationships
   /communications/associations — per Human Rights Watch's 2019 reverse-engineering.
6. Cambridge Analytica claimed ~30 million derived personality profiles; the Facebook
   data breach was first reported at 50 million accounts and later revised to ~87 million.
7. Huawei "Safe City" surveillance systems have been deployed to ~700+ cities across
   ~100+ countries (assess how much of this traces to Huawei's own marketing).
8. The TSA SPOT behavior-detection program: a GAO review found 28 of 36 behavioral
   indicators lacked valid supporting evidence; the program cost $1bn+; ~400 studies
   find human deception-detection from behaviour is roughly chance.
9. Soviet "sluggish schizophrenia": at peak it accounted for up to ~38% of Soviet
   schizophrenia diagnoses; 1,000+ politically motivated internments are documented;
   the Soviet psychiatric association withdrew from the World Psychiatric Association
   in 1983 under expulsion pressure.
10. Michael Hayden (ex-NSA/CIA) said "we kill people based on metadata"; a Stanford
    study (Mayer & Mutchler) showed phone metadata alone reliably infers things like
    religion, medical conditions, and firearm ownership.
11. Centola et al. (2018, Science) found a committed ~25% minority can flip a social
    convention; Chenoweth's NAVCO data (1900–2006) found no campaign with ≥3.5% active
    participation failed. Confirm both numbers AND that they measure different things.
```
